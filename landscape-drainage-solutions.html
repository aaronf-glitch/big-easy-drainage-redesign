Landscape drainage solutions

Which Drainage Solution Fits Your New Orleans Yard?

At Big Easy Drainage, we install five landscape drainage systems for homes and businesses across New Orleans. Each one solves a different water problem, from low spots and driveway runoff to wet crawl spaces. We assess your property first, then recommend the system that fits it, with a free estimate.

Catch Basins

A grated collection box set in a low-lying area. Water enters the grate, collects in the basin and leaves through a sloped pipe.

Catch basin installation

Channel Drains

A shallow linear trench with a removable grate that collects sheet flow on driveways, patios, pool areas and walkways.

Channel drain installation

Dry Wells

An underground pit of gravel or crushed stone that collects runoff and lets it soak slowly into the surrounding soil.

Dry well installation

French Drains

A perforated pipe in a gravel-filled trench that catches water before it reaches your foundation and carries it away.

French drain installation

Sump Pumps

A pump in a sump pit at the lowest point of the home that removes water from basements and crawl spaces.

Sump pump installation

Why it matters here

Why Do New Orleans Properties Need Landscape Drainage?

New Orleans sits below sea level near the Mississippi River and Lake Pontchartrain, and it averages more than 60 inches of rain a year. Without a planned path for that water, heavy rain pools on yards, erodes soil and can damage foundations, so reliable drainage protects both homes and businesses.

Pooling and Flooding

Low-lying ground and heavy rain leave standing water on lawns, driveways and patios.

Soil Erosion

Water moving across the yard washes soil out of beds and gardens and wears down the landscape.

Foundation Damage

Water that collects near the house can compromise the structure and lead to costly repairs.

FAQ

Landscape Drainage Questions From New Orleans Homeowners

These are the questions we hear most before a drainage project starts. If yours is not here, call us or request a free quote and we will answer it when we look at your property.

What drainage systems do you install?

We install five landscape drainage systems in New Orleans: catch basins, channel drains, dry wells, French drains and sump pumps. We look at where water collects on your property and recommend the system that fits it.

How much does a drainage system cost in New Orleans?

The cost depends on the type and size of the system, how complex the installation is and the specific needs of your property. We give you a detailed quotation after we assess your yard, and the estimate is free.

Which drainage system is best for my property?

It depends on your property's size, the volume of rainwater you expect, where the water collects and your budget. For channel drains, the foot or vehicle traffic the area gets matters too. We assess your yard and suggest the most suitable system.

How long does drainage installation take?

It depends on the scope of the project and the system you choose. A standard residential channel drain usually takes a day or two, while larger commercial projects take longer. We give you a timeline after we assess your property.

How often should a drainage system be maintained?

Catch basins should be maintained at least twice a year, and more often if your property gets heavy rain or a lot of debris. Dry wells and French drains also need regular checks for clogs and sediment.

Do you maintain drainage systems after installation?

Yes. We offer aftercare that includes routine maintenance and cleaning, so your drains stay efficient. We also diagnose and repair drainage systems that are not working as they should.

Does every New Orleans property need a drainage system?

Not every property requires one. With New Orleans' frequent heavy rain and low-lying ground, though, many properties benefit from a system that moves water away before it causes pooling, erosion or foundation damage.

Do you install drainage for commercial properties?

Yes. We install drainage systems for both residential and commercial properties in New Orleans, from yards and patios to driveways and parking lots.
