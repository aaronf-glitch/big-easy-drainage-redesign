Drainage in Slidell

Why Does Drainage Matter So Much in Slidell, LA?

Yards in Slidell sit low, at about 10 feet above sea level, and the flat ground gives rain little slope to run off. With about 66 inches of rain a year and poorly drained soils, the city relies mostly on gravity drainage, which has limited capacity.

Heavy Yearly Rainfall

NOAA 1991 to 2020 climate normals list 66.17 inches of rain a year at the Slidell station.

Low Elevation

USGS data lists Slidell at 10 feet above sea level, with a range from 3 to 27 feet across the area.

Poorly Drained Soil

A FEMA review of the W-14 drainage basin found Myatt fine sandy loam in its study area. That soil is very deep and poorly drained.

Flat Ground, Gravity Drainage

The same FEMA review calls the basin fairly flat, which makes stormwater hard to move. Most Slidell stormwater is handled by gravity drainage.

About the city

About Slidell

Here is a quick look at where Slidell sits, how it started and how many people live there.

Southeast St. Tammany Parish

Slidell sits at the southeastern tip of St. Tammany Parish. It begins where Lake Pontchartrain ends, on the Mississippi state line.

A Railroad Town Since 1882

Slidell was founded in 1882 while a new railroad was built from New Orleans to Meridian, Mississippi. It officially became a town on November 13, 1888.

Population and Land Area

The 2020 Census counted 28,781 people in Slidell, and the July 1, 2025 estimate is 28,341. The city covers 15.04 square miles of land.

Around town

Things To Do

Slidell mixes small-town history with easy access to wild swamp country.

Slidell Museum

The Slidell Museum fills the first floor of the old town hall at 2020 First Street. Its old jail cells have held no prisoners since the 1950s, and admission is free.

Olde Towne Murals

More than 17 murals by local artists decorate parts of Olde Towne. The historic train depot nearby houses Lori's Art Depot.

Christmas Under the Stars

The city hosts Christmas Under the Stars at Heritage Park along Bayou Lane. The event features life-size Christmas cottages, live entertainment and free admission.

Pearl River Wildlife Area

The Pearl River Wildlife Management Area covers 35,619 acres about 6 miles east of Slidell. Visitors come for fishing, canoeing, hiking, photography and birding.

Nearby

Nearby Suburbs

These nearby communities are measured center to center from Census Bureau map coordinates.

Mandeville

Mandeville sits about 20 miles west-northwest of Slidell. An 1887 rail line once linked the two towns along the north shore.

Covington

Covington lies about 24 miles northwest of Slidell. It sits farther north than both Mandeville and Madisonville.

Madisonville

Madisonville is about 24 miles west-northwest of Slidell. Its 1838 lighthouse guided boats across Lake Pontchartrain to the Tchefuncte River.

Gretna

Gretna lies about 31 miles south-southwest of Slidell. It sits farther south than any other city on this list.

Kenner

Kenner is about 34 miles southwest of Slidell. It sits roughly 14 miles west-northwest of Gretna.

Did you know

Interesting and Fun Facts

A few things about Slidell's past that surprise many newcomers.

Named for a Senator

Baron Erlanger named the town for his late father-in-law, John Slidell. John Slidell was active in state, national and Confederate politics.

First Lake Pontchartrain Bridge

The railroad that built Slidell also built a bridge over Lake Pontchartrain. The city notes it was the first bridge to cross the lake.

The NASA Years

A large NASA computer complex was built in Slidell. More than 15,000 NASA employees and their families arrived in the early 1960s.

A Museum Above the Museum

The old town hall dates to 1907, when brick replaced the first wooden building. Its second floor holds the Slidell Mardi Gras Museum.

Getting around

Public Transportation

Slidell riders have a parish ride service and a passenger rail stop in Olde Towne.

STAR Transit

St. Tammany Parish runs STAR Transit, a demand-response service within parish boundaries. It is open to all parish residents for curb-to-curb trips.

Booking a STAR Ride

Riders reserve trips by calling 985.327.0185 on a first-come, first-served basis. Urban service runs 7:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m., Monday through Friday.

Amtrak at Front Street

Amtrak's Crescent stops at the Slidell station at 1827 Front Street. The depot was built around 1903 and renovated in the 1990s.
