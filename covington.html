About the city

About Covington

A short look at how this north shore town began and who lives here now.

Founded as Wharton

New Orleans merchant John Wharton Collins bought the land on March 16, 1813, and planned a town named Wharton. In 1816 the state chartered it as Covington, honoring General Leonard Covington of the War of 1812.

St. Tammany Parish Seat

Covington was formally incorporated and renamed in 1816. It became the seat of St. Tammany Parish in 1829, and the parish itself dates to 1810.

Population and Land Area

The 2020 Census counted 11,564 people in Covington, and the July 1, 2025 estimate is 11,721. The city covers 8.02 square miles of land.

Around town

Things To Do

Four ways to spend a day in downtown Covington and along its rivers.

Covington Trailhead

The Covington Trailhead is a cultural center with a museum and an outdoor amphitheater. It also serves as a way station for the Tammany Trace recreational trail.

Three Rivers Art Festival

Since 1997, the Three Rivers Art Festival has filled downtown Covington streets with music, color and art each November. It has grown from 49 to 200 exhibiting artists.

Bogue Falaya Park

Bogue Falaya Park at 213 Park Drive has a paddler's launch onto the river. Visitors can picnic on its beaches or slip a kayak into the water.

Sunset at the Landing

This riverside concert series plays at the foot of Columbia Street, where it meets the Bogue Falaya River. Shows run on the third Friday of each month from March to October.

Did you know

Interesting and Fun Facts

Stories from Covington's past that still show up around town.

The Ozone Cure

During the 1878 yellow fever outbreak in New Orleans, people believed in the healing power of the north shore's ozone waters. Many came to Covington hotels to bathe and take "the cure."

Ox Lots Downtown

The original town plan set aside interior plots on each block where farmers sheltered their ox teams and wagons. That pattern earned the District of St. John a place on the National Register of Historic Places.

Walker Percy's Hometown

Author Dr. Walker Percy moved to Covington in 1950 and won the National Book Award for The Moviegoer. He wrote most of his works here until his death in 1990.

The Southern Hotel

The restored Southern Hotel on Boston Street dates to about 1907. Covington marked its bicentennial anniversary in 2013.

Getting around

Public Transportation

Covington has no fixed-route city bus, so travel runs through a parish on-demand service and a regional trail.

STAR Transit

STAR Transit is St. Tammany Parish's demand response system, open to all parish residents. It offers curb-to-curb rides on a call-in, first come, first served basis.

STAR Hours and Fares

Urban service runs 7:00 am to 5:00 pm on weekdays, and rural service ends at 4:00 pm. One-way fares start at $1.50 for trips up to 10 miles.

Tammany Trace

The Tammany Trace is a hike and bike trail on a former Illinois Central Railroad corridor. It runs from downtown Covington through Abita Springs, Mandeville and Lacombe to Slidell.
